SELECTIVE ATTENTION

Cherry (1953)

Dichotic Listening Test

Dichotic listening presents a different spoken message to each ear at the same time. It is used to study selective attention: how we focus on one source of information while filtering out another.

Cherry asked participants to shadow one message by repeating it aloud while ignoring the message in the other ear. They could recall little of the unattended message, although they often noticed basic physical changes in it.

Cherry (1953)

Cherry used dichotic listening and shadowing to investigate selective attention. Participants repeated the attended message aloud and were then asked what they had noticed in the unattended message.

This is a simplified teaching activity rather than a standardised experiment or clinical assessment.
